# Brief Encounters / Короткие встречи

**Year:** 1968 (released 1986 under Glasnost)
**Director:** Kira Muratova (also stars as Valentina)
**Period:** Late Thaw / early [[Brezhnev Stagnation]]
**Runtime:** 87 min

## Plot
Valentina is a senior city water official in a Soviet town. She loves Maxim, a geologist who is constantly absent on expeditions. Nadia, a peasant girl, also loves Maxim — she comes to work as Valentina's housekeeper without revealing the connection. The film cycles through fragmented flashbacks of all three. Maxim exists only on a tape recorder Nadia eventually erases. Banned 1968 for showing Soviet inefficiency (water shortages) and because of Vysotsky's dissident reputation.

## Concepts
- [[Characterness]] (Muratova) — Boris Groys's term, applied by Prokhorov. Valentina cycles through identities (lover, surrogate mother, water official) without ever cohering
- [[Narrative Discontinuity]] — Muratova's signature
- [[Dear Comrades Greeting]] — "Dorogie tovarishchi!" rehearsed alone
- [[Just an ordinary baba]] — hairdresser scene
- Two-women household — Attwood: Valya + Nadya's domestic routine mimics a "traditional marriage" between two women
- Vladimir Vysotsky as Maxim — bard, dissident reputation; contributed to film's banning
- Banned 1968 → released 1986 under [[Glasnost]]
