# Ivan's Childhood / Иваново детство

**Year:** 1962
**Director:** Andrei Tarkovsky (debut feature)
**Period:** [[Khrushchev Thaw]] / late Thaw
**Runtime:** 84 min
**Award:** Golden Lion, Venice 1962

## Plot
12-year-old orphan Ivan serves as a Red Army scout behind German lines. He is captured and executed. The film is punctuated by sun-drenched dream sequences (shot on negative stock) of his pre-war childhood — the well, the apple cart, the beach. Tarkovsky's signature "associative linking" replaces narrative continuity. Source story: Vladimir Bogomolov, "Ivan" (1957).

## Concepts
- [[Associative Linking]] — Tarkovsky's editing principle. Examples: well dream, apple cart, negative-stock beach scenes
- [[Sculpting in Time]] — Tarkovsky's filmmaking philosophy
- [[Child Hero]] — Tarkovsky DEFORMS the Thaw figure. Ivan is "hatred-driven" (Woll via Prokhorov); his soul is older than his body
- War as private apocalypse vs. Fall of Berlin's public victory vs. Cranes' melodrama
- Albrecht Dürer's *Four Horsemen of the Apocalypse* in Ivan's book
- Negative-stock dream sequences — scorched film
- Mother as ruin (vs. Stalinist [[Mother Russia]])
