# Bed and Sofa / Третья Мещанская

**Year:** 1926/27
**Director:** Abram Room
**Period:** [[NEP]]
**Runtime:** 72 min

## Plot
Moscow housing-shortage ménage à trois: construction worker Kolia, his wife Liuda, and Kolia's friend Volodia (a printer) sharing a single apartment. Liuda gets pregnant; uncertain who the father is; considers an abortion (just legalized under NEP) but ultimately rejects both men, leaves Moscow, keeps the child.

## Concepts
- [[Meshchanstvo]] — petty-bourgeois philistinism; the Russian title (Tret'ia Meshchanskaia) names the street AND the moral disease
- [[Bolshevik Gender Line]] — 1918 Family Code, 1920 abortion legalization, Kollontai's "winged Eros" / "glass of water" theory
- Spatial gender hierarchy — Liuda's position in the apartment encodes her status (the sofa, the bed, the kitchen)
- Abortion on screen — Liuda considers it (just legalized under NEP), then chooses to leave both men
- [[NEP]] — the policy frame that allowed this film
