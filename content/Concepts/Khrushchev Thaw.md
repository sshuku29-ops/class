# Khrushchev Thaw

**Period:** February 1956 (Khrushchev's [[Khrushchev Secret Speech]]) to August 1968 (Soviet invasion of Czechoslovakia)
**Author/Source:** Concept named after Ehrenburg's 1954 novella *The Thaw*; theorized by Alexander Prokhorov in "Springtime for Soviet Cinema"

## Definition
Post-Stalin liberalization. Releases of GULAG prisoners; cultural opening; permission for individual subjectivity in art. Cinema enters its New Wave moment. Ends abruptly when Soviet tanks crush the Prague Spring.

## Films using it
- [[09 The Cranes Are Flying]] (1957) — the inaugural Thaw film
- [[10 Ivan's Childhood]] (1962) — the Thaw at its philosophical depth
- [[11 Brief Encounters]] (1968) — Thaw's terminus; banned the same year the Thaw ends

## Example
The Thaw permits what Stalinism repressed: female subjectivity ([[Femininity]]), wartime trauma ([[Trench Truth]]), individual interiority ([[Soviet New Wave]]). Veronika in Cranes is the Thaw's signature — flawed, traumatized, refusing the Stakhanovite-mother model. The Thaw is the precondition for everything that follows in Soviet cinema.
