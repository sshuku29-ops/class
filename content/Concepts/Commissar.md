# Commissar 🎯

> **🎯 STUDY SHEET CONCEPT** — listed under [[06 Chapaev]] on the prof's exam study sheet. Part I-B Option 1 candidate.

## Source

Soviet political-military institution dating from the Civil War (1918–22). Discussed in the Chapaev lecture and in **Katerina Clark**, *The Soviet Novel: History as Ritual* (1981) as the canonical "elder brother" / mentor figure of the [[Spontaneity-Consciousness Dialectic]].

## Definition

A commissar (комиссар) was a Communist Party political officer attached to a Red Army unit during the Civil War, responsible for the political-ideological education of soldiers and for ensuring the unit's loyalty to the party. Each Red Army commander was paired with a commissar, with dual command structure: the commander handled tactics, the commissar handled ideology. In Clark's framework, the commissar in cinema and literature is the canonical embodiment of the elder-brother / father figure who delivers the spontaneous hero into Bolshevik consciousness.

## Key context

The commissar is the institutional bridge between Stalin-as-Father and the masses-as-children in Clark's [[Big Soviet Family]] hierarchy. The dual command structure (commander + commissar) parallels the [[Spontaneity-Consciousness Dialectic]] (raw talent + ideological clarity) — the commissar IS the dialectic in human institutional form. After the Civil War, the commissar role evolved into the political officer (политрук) and was eventually abolished, but the figure persists as a recurring character in Soviet cinema.

## Films using it

- 🎯 [[06 Chapaev]] (1934) — Furmanov is THE filmic commissar

## Concrete example (write this in your exam answer)

In *Chapaev* (1934), Furmanov arrives at Chapaev's unit and finds a brilliant but ideologically formless peasant commander. Through patient pedagogical scenes — most famously the "potato lesson" where Furmanov uses potatoes on a table to teach Chapaev where commanders should position themselves in battle — he turns Chapaev from a Civil War natural talent into a Bolshevik-conscious revolutionary. Furmanov is the master plot's elder brother in concrete institutional form: the commissar is what makes the [[Spontaneity-Consciousness Dialectic]] narratively possible, and through him Chapaev enters the [[Big Soviet Family]].
