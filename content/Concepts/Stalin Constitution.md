# Stalin Constitution (1936)

**Source:** USSR Constitution adopted December 1936; foundational Stalinist state document

## Definition
Proclaimed "the most democratic constitution in the world." Declared universal suffrage, gender equality, and full social rights — all on paper while the Great Terror was about to begin. Functions as the supreme example of Stalinist [[Modal Schizophrenia]]: equality "as it will be" while reality is purges and famine.

## Films using it
- [[07 Volga-Volga]] (1938) — celebrates the constitutional moment cinematically
- [[14 Burnt by the Sun]] — film set June 1936, two months before the first show trial; the Constitution had just been declared

## Example
The Constitution promised gender equality at the exact moment the state was banning abortion (1936) and reinstating the cult of motherhood. Volga-Volga's Strelka — the postwoman who writes a winning song and is feted in Moscow — visualizes the Constitution's promise of female equality. The reality (Strelka's authorship dissolves into male display) is the film's [[Modal Schizophrenia]].
