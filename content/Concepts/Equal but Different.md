# "Equal but Different"

**Authors:** Lynne Attwood; Igor Kon (concept)
**Source:** Attwood, "Brezhnev: Era of Stagnation"; Kon, "Gender and Sexuality in Russia"

## Definition
Attwood's exact phrasing: "equality redefined so that it was not incompatible with women putting work in second place and devoting more time to the family." The 1970s redefinition of Soviet gender equality, driven by [[Pro-family Campaign]] / [[Demographic Crisis]]. Igor Kon names this as the late-Soviet gender ideology.

## Films using it
- [[12 Moscow Doesn't Believe in Tears]] — Gosha's "I'm the man" speech is its purest expression

## Example
Gosha's discovery that Katerina out-earns him triggers his crisis; the film "resolves" it by showing Katerina submitting her professional success to be "ordinary housewife" (Zorkaya cited by Attwood). The audience laughs and cheers. "Equal but Different" is the ideological frame that makes this submission look like love.
