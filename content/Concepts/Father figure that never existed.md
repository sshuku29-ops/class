# "Father figure that never existed" 📚

> **📚 SUPPLEMENTARY** — appears in assigned readings or lectures, useful for the essay (Option 2) and for shot analysis (Parts II + III). Not directly named on the prof's exam study sheet, so unlikely to appear as a Part I-B Option 1 question.


**Author:** Susan Larsen
**Source:** "National Identity, Cultural Authority, and the Post-Soviet Blockbuster: Nikita Mikhalkov and Aleksei Balabanov," *Slavic Review* 62.3 (Fall 2003), p. 493

## Definition
Mikhalkov constructs Kotov as a heroic Red commander to give post-Soviet Russia a new father — but the figure is always already destroyed by the system that produced him. The post-Soviet attempt to restore patriarchal Soviet authority retroactively fails because the authority was never coherent in the first place.

## Films using it
- [[14 Burnt by the Sun]] — Larsen's analytical category for the film

## Example
Mikhalkov assembles the perfect Stalinist patriarchal family — Red Army father, intelligentsia wife, daughter named Hope — precisely so the Stalinist system can destroy it. The "father figure" Mikhalkov constructs in the river-idyll opening (you Sprint Burnt Shot 1) is the figure Larsen names: it never actually existed historically; it exists only as the post-Soviet film's elegiac construction.
