# Male Gaze

**Author:** Laura Mulvey
**Source:** "Visual Pleasure and Narrative Cinema" (*Screen* journal, 1975)

## Definition
Classical narrative cinema codes the look as active/male, the looked-at as passive/female. Camera, male protagonist, and spectator share one masculine gaze. Women are styled for "to-be-looked-at-ness." The prof names Mulvey explicitly on the exam study sheet — required theorist.

## Example
The Mark assault scene in Cranes Are Flying is a textbook Mulvey scene: low-angle Medium Close-Up with Mark as a dark mass pressing in from the left, Veronika's face the only lit object. The camera's gaze structures Mark's possession; subtitle contradiction ("I love you!" / "No!") makes the script-vs-image dynamic explicit.
