# Pro-family Campaign / Demographic Crisis 📚

> **📚 SUPPLEMENTARY** — appears in assigned readings or lectures, useful for the essay (Option 2) and for shot analysis (Parts II + III). Not directly named on the prof's exam study sheet, so unlikely to appear as a Part I-B Option 1 question.


**Period:** 1970s (sharpened after 1979 Afghanistan invasion)
**Author:** Lynne Attwood

## Definition
1970s Brezhnev-era state response to falling birth rates in European Soviet republics combined with high birth rates in Central Asia (Russian numerical supremacy under threat; growing Muslim Red Army share politically sensitive after 1979 Afghanistan war). Campaign through press articles, school course on "Ethics and Psychology of Family Life," part-time work laws for mothers.

## Films using it
- [[12 Moscow Doesn't Believe in Tears]] (1979) — the film's pro-traditional gender messaging is part of this campaign

## Example
Moscow Doesn't Believe in Tears arrives at the peak of the pro-family campaign. Katerina's two-decade arc (1958 → 1979) doesn't end with her at her professional peak; it ends with her watching Gosha eat in the kitchen, in tears. The film's ideological function is the campaign in dramatic form: the woman's ideal endpoint is the kitchen, the husband, the family.
