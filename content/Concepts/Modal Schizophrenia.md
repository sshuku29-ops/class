# Modal Schizophrenia

**Author:** Katerina Clark (term); [[Lunacharsky's SR Definition]] (concept origin)
**Source:** *The Soviet Novel: History as Ritual* (1981)

## Definition
[[Socialist Realism]] portrays reality "as it should be" — a fusion of present "is" and future "ought." Reality and dream coexist in the same frame. Lunacharsky's exact words (Film Factory p. 327): the SR artist "does not accept reality as reality is. He accepts it as it will be."

## Films using it
- [[06 Chapaev]] — Civil War depicted with future-Soviet ideological clarity
- [[07 Volga-Volga]] — clearest example. Moscow appears as it will be (utopian abundance), not as it was in 1938
- [[08 The Fall of Berlin]] — Stalin appears as the divine architect of victory, not the leader who lost millions

## Example
Volga-Volga's Moscow shines with abundance — clean streets, gleaming Stalinist architecture, joyful Stakhanovite citizens — at the exact moment the actual 1938 Moscow was running on rationing, terror, and forced labor. The film is Modal Schizophrenia: the camera shows what reality "will be," fusing present and ideological future.
