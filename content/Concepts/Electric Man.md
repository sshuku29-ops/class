# Electric Man / Perfect Electric Man 🎯

## Source

**Dziga Vertov**, "WE: Variant of a Manifesto" (МЫ. Вариант манифеста), originally published in *Kinofot* 1 (Moscow, 1922); reprinted in **Kino-Eye: The Writings of Dziga Vertov** (ed. Annette Michelson, trans. Kevin O'Brien, 1984).

## Definition

Vertov's "Electric Man" is the Constructivist-utopian fusion of the human body with mechanical apparatus into a single optimized organism — the new Soviet subject. Vertov writes: "I am the Kino-Eye. I am a mechanical eye. I, a machine, show you the world as only I can see it… My path leads to the creation of a fresh perception of the world. Thus I explain in a new way the world unknown to you." The Electric Man is the cinematic embodiment of Soviet machine-age modernity: the worker who is part-machine, the camera that is part-human, the Soviet body that operates at the speed and precision of industrial production.

## Key context

The Electric Man is Vertov's positive program — the [[Kino-Eye]]'s political payoff. Where bourgeois cinema films "personalities," Vertov's revolutionary cinema films humans-becoming-Electric-Men: the worker fused with the lathe, the woman fused with the typewriter, the eye fused with the camera shutter. The concept belongs to the broader 1920s Constructivist project (Rodchenko, Lissitzky, Tatlin) of remaking Soviet daily life through industrial design.

## Concrete example (write this in your exam answer)

In *Man with a Movie Camera* (1929), Vertov constructs the Electric Man through two key parallels: (1) a woman washing her face is intercut with city machinery being washed by hose-jets — domestic and industrial cleansing equated as parallel forms of revolutionary hygiene; (2) the woman blinks and adjusts her vision while the camera shutter focuses — human eye and mechanical eye fused into a single perceptual organ. Vertov INTENDS the woman as active participant in the Electric Man synthesis; for the gender-equality essay track, the contradiction the camera leaks is that the apparatus's voyeuristic positioning (the Mulveyan [[Male Gaze]]) undermines Vertov's stated egalitarian project — but the Electric Man is the framework Vertov is working in.
