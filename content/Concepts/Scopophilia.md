# Scopophilia

**Author:** Laura Mulvey (via Freud)
**Source:** "Visual Pleasure and Narrative Cinema" (1975)

## Definition
Pleasure in looking. Cinema's voyeuristic dispositive — dark room, lit screen, audience invisible to actors — institutionalizes scopophilia. Mulvey's diagnostic: cinema-going IS a structurally voyeuristic act, and classical Hollywood narrative organizes this voyeurism around the female body.

## Films using it
- [[03 Man with a Movie Camera]] — Vertov's peeping-camera scene through tree branches is unintentional Soviet scopophilia
- [[09 The Cranes Are Flying]] — the Mark assault stages scopophilia within the diegesis
- [[14 Burnt by the Sun]] — Mitia's gaze through the doorway at Marusia is structured scopophilia

## Example
The opening of MWAMC films a woman waking through her window. Vertov's stated philosophy ([[Electric Man]]) wants her as active participant. Mulvey's framework reveals what the camera position does: institutionalizes scopophilic distance. The viewer is positioned in the Freudian voyeur seat even as Vertov's manifesto disclaims it.
