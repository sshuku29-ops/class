# To-be-looked-at-ness

**Author:** Laura Mulvey
**Source:** "Visual Pleasure and Narrative Cinema" (1975)

## Definition
The coded female function in mainstream narrative cinema — to freeze the action and become spectacle. Mulvey: women on screen are styled, lit, and framed to display their function as objects of the male gaze; their appearance halts the narrative drive.

## Films using it
- [[01 Daydreams]] — Tina as performance
- [[09 The Cranes Are Flying]] — Veronika in the Mark scene; her lit face as the frame's only "to-be-looked-at" surface
- [[13 Little Vera]] — Vera deliberately staged as object of the male gaze (perestroika sexual desublimation)
- [[14 Burnt by the Sun]] — Marusia in the doorway during the Mitia piano scene

## Example
In Burnt by the Sun, Marusia stands in the doorway while Mitia plays piano. Her body bisected vertically by the threshold; lit face the camera's reverse-shot subject. She does not act in this scene — she is to-be-looked-at, by Mitia and by the viewer. The film's gender critique works by making this structure visible.
