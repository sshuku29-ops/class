# Isaak Dunaevsky 🎯

> **🎯 STUDY SHEET CONCEPT** — listed under [[07 Volga-Volga]] on the prof's exam study sheet. Part I-B Option 1 candidate.

## Source

**Isaak Osipovich Dunaevsky** (1900–1955), Soviet film composer. Discussed in the Volga-Volga lecture and in **Richard Stites**, *Russian Popular Culture* (Cambridge, 1992) — Stites is DL-flagged but the historical material on Dunaevsky is uncontested. **Maya Turovskaya**'s scholarship on Stalinist musical comedy (in the Stalin's Cinema reader) is the assigned secondary source.

## Definition

Isaak Dunaevsky was the composer behind the canonical Stalinist musical comedies of the 1930s, working in close collaboration with director **Grigorii Aleksandrov** to produce the "Soviet Hollywood" musical genre. Dunaevsky's melodies — bright, brassy, irresistibly hummable — became the era's earworms; "March of the Enthusiasts" from *Bright Path* (1940) functioned as a quasi-anthem. His music is the sonic register of Stalinist [[Modal Schizophrenia]]: utopian abundance in melodic form.

## Key context

Dunaevsky + Aleksandrov is the Soviet equivalent of Hollywood's Berkeley + Goldwyn — a director-composer team producing genre cinema for mass audiences. Their major collaborations: *Jolly Fellows* (Веселые ребята, 1934), *The Circus* (Цирк, 1936), *Volga-Volga* (1938), *Bright Path* (Светлый путь, 1940). Stalin reportedly sent a copy of Volga-Volga to Roosevelt as a goodwill gesture during WWII; the film was Stalin's personal favorite. Dunaevsky's music is functionally part of the propaganda apparatus — its relentless cheerfulness IS the [[Magic Tablecloth]] in melodic form.

## Concrete example (write this in your exam answer)

In *Volga-Volga* (1938), Dunaevsky's title theme — performed in the film as a song-within-the-song authored by Strelka — became one of the most-recognized melodies in Soviet culture. The melody's irresistible cheerfulness covers the film's structural contradiction: Strelka's authorship is celebrated as Stakhanovite female achievement while the orchestrated mass-performance dissolves her individual contribution into the spectacle of Soviet collective joy. Dunaevsky's score is the [[Magic Tablecloth]] at the level of sound: musical abundance covering political reality, Soviet joy "as it will be" rather than as the rationed and surveilled 1938 actually was.
