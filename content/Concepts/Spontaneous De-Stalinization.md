# Spontaneous De-Stalinization 📚

> **📚 SUPPLEMENTARY** — appears in assigned readings or lectures, useful for the essay (Option 2) and for shot analysis (Parts II + III). Not directly named on the prof's exam study sheet, so unlikely to appear as a Part I-B Option 1 question.


**Author:** Richard Stites
**Source:** *Russian Popular Culture* (DL-flagged; concept available via prof's lecture)

## Definition
The "more uncertain and chaotic early war years" (1941–42) when military catastrophe forced Soviet citizens onto their own resources, breaking Stalinist controls before Khrushchev's official de-Stalinization. Personal initiative briefly returned. Reversed after the Stalingrad victory (Feb 2, 1943).

## Films using it
- Conceptual frame for understanding [[09 The Cranes Are Flying]]'s Thaw critique of Stalinist war culture
- Backdrop to [[10 Ivan's Childhood]]'s war-as-private-apocalypse

## Example
The 1941–42 chaos was when ordinary Soviets had to act without waiting for Party orders — partisan organizing, evacuation, improvised production. Stalin's "Brothers and sisters" speech was the official acknowledgment. After Stalingrad, the regime reasserted vertical authority and erased this period from official memory. Cranes Are Flying restores it.
