# Stakhanov and Stakhanovites / Stakhanovite Movement 🎯

> **🎯 STUDY SHEET CONCEPT** — listed under [[07 Volga-Volga]] on the prof's exam study sheet. Part I-B Option 1 candidate.

## Source

State-orchestrated labor productivity campaign launched by Stalin in 1935. Aleksei Stakhanov's name became the eponym for the movement. **Aleksei Stakhanov, "My Suggestion to Soviet Cinema,"** in *The Film Factory*, ed. Richard Taylor and Ian Christie. Discussed in the Volga-Volga lecture.

## Definition

The Stakhanovite Movement is the cult of the over-fulfilling worker. On 31 August 1935, the Donbass coal miner **Aleksei Stakhanov** supposedly mined 102 tons of coal in a single 6-hour shift — fourteen times his quota. The Soviet state used Stakhanov's record (the actual feat was orchestrated by his crew, with Stakhanov as the photogenic face) as the founding moment for a national propaganda campaign to legitimize industrial speedup. "Stakhanovites" became a state-recognized class of exemplary workers, with material privileges, public honors, and propaganda roles.

## Key context

The Stakhanovite Movement is part of the same 1935–36 ideological consolidation that produces the [[Stalin Constitution]] (1936) and the [[Anti-abortion Law 1936]]. Together, these projects define the late-1930s Stalinist Big Family: the heroic Soviet citizen is the Stakhanovite worker AND the dutiful mother, exemplary in both production and reproduction. The movement extended beyond industrial labor to agriculture, arts, and entertainment — Stakhanovite singers, Stakhanovite tractor drivers, Stakhanovite kindergarten teachers became propaganda figures. Stakhanov's own essay "My Suggestion to Soviet Cinema" calls for cinema to celebrate his movement, which Aleksandrov directly answers.

## Films using it

- 🎯 [[07 Volga-Volga]] (1938) — Strelka's amateur-to-Stakhanovite arc

## Concrete example (write this in your exam answer)

In *Volga-Volga* (1938), Strelka the postwoman writes a hit song called "Volga-Volga" in her spare time and travels to Moscow with her amateur troupe to compete in the All-Union Music Olympiad — Stakhanovite excess applied to amateur arts. The film celebrates the Stakhanovite principle that every Soviet citizen should be exemplary, beyond their assigned role. Strelka is the ideological subject the [[Stalin Constitution]] promises and the Stakhanovite Movement teaches: a small-town postal worker who, through pure Soviet enthusiasm, produces a hit song that wins national recognition. The film's [[Modal Schizophrenia]] is that this enthusiasm is presented as universal Soviet reality at the moment most Soviet workers were on rationing and surveillance.
