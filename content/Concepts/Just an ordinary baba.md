# "Just an ordinary baba" / просто баба

**Source:** [[11 Brief Encounters]] (1968), hairdresser scene
**Author:** Lynne Attwood (analysis)

## Definition
The hairdresser dismisses Valentina (a senior city water official) as a "baba" — a colloquial Russian term that combines "old woman" / "mare" / "common dame." Attwood: even at the top of local government, a woman is reduced by class-and-gender contempt. Visualizes Brezhnev-era retreat from women's equality.

## Films using it
- [[11 Brief Encounters]] — exclusive scene

## Example
Valentina has reached a senior post in city government — a triumph of Soviet women's equality on paper. The hairdresser's dismissal — "she's just a baba" — registers what late-Soviet society actually thinks. Attwood reads this as one of the film's central indictments of [[Brezhnev Stagnation]]'s gender ideology.
