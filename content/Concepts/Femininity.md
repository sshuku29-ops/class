# Femininity (Thaw value) 📚

> **📚 SUPPLEMENTARY** — appears in assigned readings or lectures, useful for the essay (Option 2) and for shot analysis (Parts II + III). Not directly named on the prof's exam study sheet, so unlikely to appear as a Part I-B Option 1 question.


**Authors:** Alexander Prokhorov; Lynne Attwood; Evgenii Margolit
**Source:** Prokhorov, "Springtime for Soviet Cinema"; Margolit, "Landscape, with Hero"; Attwood, "Khrushchev and the Thaw"

## Definition
The prof's exact phrasing (from her PPTX): "the Thaw's 'feminization' of Soviet life: women and children became central characters." Plus "absolute value of sincerity" / "authenticity within family life and romantic love." Veronika in Cranes is the breakthrough — Margolit calls her "asymmetry of natural gesture," "plasticity akin to a plant." Breaks the Stalinist Stakhanovite-mother model.

## Films using it
- [[09 The Cranes Are Flying]] — the foundational Thaw femininity

## Example
Veronika in Cranes is the first major Soviet heroine permitted to be flawed — coerced by Mark, traumatized, contradictory, individually subjective. Stakhanovite mothers of the 1930s are exemplary types; Veronika is a person. That re-scaling of femininity from type to person IS the Thaw's gender breakthrough.
