# Life as it is / Life Caught Unawares 🎯

> **🎯 STUDY SHEET CONCEPT** — listed under [[03 Man with a Movie Camera]] on the prof's exam study sheet. Part I-B Option 1 candidate.

## Source

**Dziga Vertov**, "On Kinopravda" (1924), "The Birth of Kino-Eye" (1924), "The Vertov Council on the First Issue of Kinopravda" — reprinted in **Kino-Eye: The Writings of Dziga Vertov** (ed. Annette Michelson, trans. Kevin O'Brien, 1984).

## Definition

"Life as it is" (жизнь как она есть) and the parallel phrase "life caught unawares" (жизнь врасплох) are Vertov's terms for the documentary ethic of his Kino-Eye program: cinema's task is to film reality without staging, actors, or script — capturing what would happen even if the camera were not there. Vertov calls this "the creation of a fresh perception of the world… from a chaos of visual phenomena." Fiction film, by contrast, Vertov calls "a substitute for life that is not life."

## Key context

"Life as it is" is the anti-fiction polemic at the core of Vertov's Constructivist documentary practice. Where Eisenstein's [[Montage of Attractions]] manipulates the viewer through staged shocks, Vertov insists Soviet cinema must derive its political force from the facts of revolutionary reality itself, captured by the [[Kino-Eye]] and assembled in [[Factory of Facts]] mode. This is also a polemic against pre-Revolutionary Russian fiction film (Bauer's *Daydreams* would be Vertov's enemy par excellence) and against NEP-era imported Hollywood entertainment.

## Films using it

- 🎯 [[03 Man with a Movie Camera]] (1929) — the entire film is built on this principle

## Concrete example (write this in your exam answer)

Every sequence of *Man with a Movie Camera* (1929) demonstrates "life as it is": real workers in real factories, real women waking in real Moscow apartments, real traffic, real eye examinations, a real childbirth. Vertov's "three no's" — no actors, no intertitles, no script — are the formal corollary. The film's reflexivity (the cameraman filming the cameraman, the editor editing the editor) is not contradiction but extension: the production process itself is part of "life as it is" under socialism.
