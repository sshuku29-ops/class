# Magic Tablecloth

**Author:** Sheila Fitzpatrick
**Source:** *Everyday Stalinism* (NOTE: "Magic Tablecloth" essay was DL-assigned; Fitzpatrick's "Palaces on Monday" is the assigned text)

## Definition
Stalinist propaganda promised abundance ("Life has become better, life has become more joyous, comrades") while real shelves were empty. The Stalinist musical and Stakhanovite cinema function as the visual magic tablecloth — they make abundance appear on screen even as it is absent in everyday life.

## Films using it
- [[07 Volga-Volga]] — the magic tablecloth's purest cinematic instance
- [[08 The Fall of Berlin]] — magic tablecloth + cult of Stalin
- *The Circus* (1936) — the same Aleksandrov-Dunaevsky pattern

## Example
Volga-Volga's Moscow is a magic tablecloth: streets gleam, citizens sing, abundance is everywhere. The actual 1938 Moscow is starving on rations and being purged. The film paints the tablecloth Stalinism could not actually set.
