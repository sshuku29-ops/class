# "Burying him means forgiving him"

**Source:** *Repentance* (1984, released 1986; dir. Tengiz Abuladze) — Ketevan's line
**Period:** Foundational text of [[Cinematic Deconstruction of Stalin]]

## Definition
Ketevan's line in *Repentance*. Anti-amnesia ethic of the cinematic-deconstruction tradition. "To ignore the past is to accept it." The principle: the Soviet past must be actively reckoned with, not buried-and-forgotten.

## Films using it
- *Repentance* (1986) — original
- [[14 Burnt by the Sun]] (1994) — joins the ethical project

## Example
The line "burying him means forgiving him" comes from Repentance's plot: a daughter repeatedly digs up the corpse of a Stalinist tyrant her city wants to bury. The act of refusing burial IS political memory. Mikhalkov's Burnt by the Sun extends this ethic: the film refuses to let Stalinism be merely "past." Kotov's arrest day is filmed as if it just happened.
