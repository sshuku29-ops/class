# Melodrama 📚

> **📚 SUPPLEMENTARY** — appears in assigned readings or lectures, useful for the essay (Option 2) and for shot analysis (Parts II + III). Not directly named on the prof's exam study sheet, so unlikely to appear as a Part I-B Option 1 question.


**Author:** Louise McReynolds
**Source:** *Russia at Play / Imitations of Life: Two Centuries of Melodrama in Russia*

## Definition
Genre originating in post-1789 France to make modernity comprehensible to mass audiences. "Aesthetics of excess": inflated emotions, stylized sets, wildly unbelievable plots. "Providence and coincidence" replace reason as narrative motor. Cranes Are Flying IS Soviet melodrama.

## Films using it
- [[09 The Cranes Are Flying]] — the foundational Soviet melodrama
- [[12 Moscow Doesn't Believe in Tears]] — neo-Stalinist melodrama / Hollywood-Soviet hybrid

## Example
Cranes signals melodrama through every formal choice: inflated emotional register (Veronika's wail; the staircase climb), stylized visuals (Urusevsky's chiaroscuro), Providence-and-coincidence (Mark's appearance during the air raid). The Soviet New Wave built its first major film as melodrama precisely because melodrama lets the audience sort the ethics of a secular post-Stalinist world.
