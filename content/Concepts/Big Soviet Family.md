# Big Soviet Family / Big Family Myth

**Author:** Katerina Clark
**Source:** *The Soviet Novel: History as Ritual* (1981), pp. 114–135

## Definition
Stalinist hierarchy where Stalin is Father, party leaders are elder brothers, the people are children. Replaces the horizontal brotherhood of revolutionary 1920s. Vertical fathers-sons structure organizes all Stalinist culture. The prof's central exam framework.

## Films using it
- [[06 Chapaev]] — canonical installation
- [[07 Volga-Volga]] — installed under cult of personality
- [[09 The Cranes Are Flying]] — fractured (Boris dies; the script breaks)
- [[10 Ivan's Childhood]] — broken; orphan-producing
- [[11 Brief Encounters]] — decayed; cannot narrate itself
- [[12 Moscow Doesn't Believe in Tears]] — restored in private, kitchen-scale form
- [[14 Burnt by the Sun]] — deconstructed (Kotov as the father figure that never existed)
- [[15 The Return]] — masculinized to the point of erasing the maternal

## Example
In Chapaev, the [[Commissar]] Furmanov is the elder brother who educates the spontaneous peasant Chapaev into ideological consciousness — and behind both stands Stalin as Father. In Burnt by the Sun, Mikhalkov literalizes the hierarchy: Kotov as Father, Marusia as wife, Nadia as Hope, Stalin's portrait literally floating above them all on the dirigible.
