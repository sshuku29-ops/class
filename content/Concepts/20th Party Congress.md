# 20th Communist Party Congress 🎯

> **🎯 STUDY SHEET CONCEPT** — listed under [[09 The Cranes Are Flying]] on the prof's exam study sheet. Part I-B Option 1 candidate. **The prof asks specifically for the year AND major developments.**

## Source

Twentieth Congress of the Communist Party of the Soviet Union, held in Moscow **14–25 February 1956**. Primary historical source: **Khrushchev's "Secret Speech" of 25 February 1956**, "On the Cult of Personality and Its Consequences" (О культе личности и его последствиях). Discussed in the Cranes Are Flying lecture and in **Alexander Prokhorov**, "Springtime for Soviet Cinema."

## Definition

The 20th Party Congress is the institutional event at which Khrushchev delivered the closed-session [[Khrushchev Secret Speech]] denouncing Stalin's crimes — the show trials, the mass executions, the [[Cult of Stalin]]. The Speech was technically secret but circulated widely within months through diplomatic channels and party networks. It split Soviet society overnight into two camps: "Stalin's heirs" (those who defended the late dictator) and the "children of the Twentieth Congress" (Yevgeny Yevtushenko's term for the generation morally formed by the Speech). The Congress also formally endorsed peaceful coexistence with capitalist states and a multi-path approach to socialism.

## Key context

The 20th Party Congress is the founding moment of the [[Khrushchev Thaw]]. The Speech's denunciation of Stalin specifically attacks [[08 The Fall of Berlin|The Fall of Berlin]] (1949) for its [[Cult of Stalin]] — the Congress is therefore the institutional license for the [[Soviet New Wave]] to begin. The Speech also legitimized rehabilitation of GULAG prisoners (millions formally released or posthumously cleared in 1956–60), reopening of suppressed literary works, and a new permissibility for cinematic depiction of Soviet life that did not center Stalin or the Big Family hierarchy. The [[Shestidesyatniki]] generation is its product.

## Films using it

- 🎯 [[09 The Cranes Are Flying]] (1957) — the first major Soviet film released after the Congress
- All subsequent [[Khrushchev Thaw]] cinema is post-20th-Congress

## Concrete example (write this in your exam answer)

*The Cranes Are Flying* (1957) is unimaginable without the 20th Party Congress. The film's depiction of the Great Patriotic War — through Veronika's individual female trauma rather than Stalinist victory — would have been censored under late Stalinism. The Speech's destruction of the [[Cult of Stalin]] specifically targets the Stalinist war film tradition (*The Fall of Berlin* is named); the New Wave's [[Trench Truth]] response is a direct cinematic answer to the Congress's permission. Prokhorov frames the Soviet New Wave as the cinema of the "children of the Twentieth Congress."
