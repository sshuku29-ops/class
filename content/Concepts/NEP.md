# NEP / New Economic Policy 🎯

> **🎯 STUDY SHEET CONCEPT** — listed under [[03 Man with a Movie Camera]] on the prof's exam study sheet. Part I-B Option 1 candidate. **The prof's own model answer for this concept is on the office-hours handout — match its register.**

## Source

Soviet economic policy enacted at the Tenth Party Congress, **March 1921**, by V.I. Lenin; abandoned 1929 with the First Five-Year Plan. Encyclopedia entries follow the prof's syllabus link; for course context see the Week 2/3 reading and Bauer/Vertov/Room screening cluster.

## Definition

The New Economic Policy was Lenin's strategic retreat from War Communism after the Civil War's economic devastation. Lenin called NEP a retreat of the state to "the commanding heights of the economy" — large-scale industry, banking, foreign commerce — while permitting private trade, small businesses, and peasant land cultivation in the rest of the economy. Lenin insisted NEP had to be pursued "seriously and for a long time."

## Key context

Under NEP the Soviet economy revived: by 1926–27 most economic indices were at or near pre-war levels. But recovery via market forces was accompanied by the re-emergence of a "capitalist" class in both the countryside (the [[Kulak]]s) and the towns (NEPmen), persistent unemployment among workers, and anxieties within the party about bourgeois degeneracy and the loss of revolutionary dynamism. The triumph of Stalin over his political rivals, the adoption of the First Five-Year Plan for industrialization, and the decision to launch a "Socialist Offensive" against the kulaks effectively marked the abandonment of NEP by 1929.

## Concrete example (write this in your exam answer)

The anxiety about "bourgeois degeneracy" and "loss of revolutionary dynamism" is reflected in *Bed and Sofa* (1926/27), where the working class — represented by Kolia and Volodia — turns into bourgeois philistines, stagnating in the semi-capitalist atmosphere of NEP. The film's diagnostic of [[Meshchanstvo]] (petty-bourgeois philistinism) is NEP's cultural shadow. Vertov's *Man with a Movie Camera* (1929), made at NEP's terminus, by contrast tries to capture revolutionary dynamism through its [[Kino-Eye]] aesthetic — the [[Electric Man]] is Vertov's anti-meshchanstvo counter-program.
