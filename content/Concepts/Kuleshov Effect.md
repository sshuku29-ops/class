# Kuleshov Effect

**Author:** Lev Kuleshov (1899–1970)
**Source:** Kuleshov's experiments (early 1920s); foundational for all Soviet montage theory

## Definition
The same close-up of an actor's face, intercut with different objects (soup, child in coffin, woman), produces totally different emotional readings. Cinema's meaning lives in editing, NOT performance. The viewer constructs emotion from the cut, not from the actor.

## Films using it
- Background concept; the foundation [[02 Battleship Potemkin]] (Eisenstein), [[03 Man with a Movie Camera]] (Vertov) build on
- Implicit in any Soviet montage film

## Example
Kuleshov's classic experiment: the same neutral face of actor Mozzhukhin, cut with a bowl of soup, then a corpse in a coffin, then a woman on a sofa, was read by audiences as showing hunger, grief, and desire — though the face itself never changed.
