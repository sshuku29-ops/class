# Monumental Tableau 🎯

> **🎯 STUDY SHEET CONCEPT** — listed under [[04 Earth]] on the prof's exam study sheet. Part I-B Option 1 candidate.

## Source

Critical concept developed in Soviet film criticism around Dovzhenko's poetic cinema; discussed in the Earth lecture and in **Vance Kepley Jr., *In the Service of the State: The Cinema of Alexander Dovzhenko*** (University of Wisconsin Press, 1986).

## Definition

The Monumental Tableau is Dovzhenko's signature compositional form: a static-but-charged frame in which bodies, landscape, and ritual gesture are arranged in painterly stillness, often referencing religious iconography (icon, fresco, Pietà). Where Eisenstein's [[Montage of Attractions]] generates meaning through the cut, Dovzhenko's Monumental Tableau generates meaning through the held composition itself — the frame becomes a charged stillness in which natural cycle (death, harvest, birth) and political cycle (class struggle, collectivization, revolution) overlay.

## Key context

Critics including Sergei Eisenstein himself accused Dovzhenko of "biologism" — reducing class struggle to a natural-mythic cycle that depoliticizes the actual policy violence of collectivization. Defenders read the Monumental Tableau as Dovzhenko's distinctive Ukrainian-poetic mode, drawing on folk iconography and Orthodox visual tradition to produce a non-Eisensteinian form of revolutionary cinema. The Monumental Tableau anticipates Tarkovsky's later [[Sculpting in Time]] aesthetic.

## Films using it

- 🎯 [[04 Earth]] (1930) — the foundational instance

## Concrete example (write this in your exam answer)

In *Earth* (1930), the grandfather's death is staged in Monumental Tableau: the dying old man lies under a heavily-laden apple tree, surrounded by family and ripe fruit, in a static composition that reads as religious icon. The tableau treats death as biological-spiritual ritual rather than narrative event. A second example: Vasyl's mother gives birth on screen at the moment of Vasyl's funeral, the two events composed in parallel tableaux that bind death and renewal into Dovzhenko's "biologistic" cycle. The visual stillness substitutes for Eisensteinian montage as the meaning-producing form.
