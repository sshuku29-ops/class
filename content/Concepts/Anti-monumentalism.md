# Anti-monumentalism 📚

> **📚 SUPPLEMENTARY** — appears in assigned readings or lectures, useful for the essay (Option 2) and for shot analysis (Parts II + III). Not directly named on the prof's exam study sheet, so unlikely to appear as a Part I-B Option 1 question.


**Author:** Alexander Prokhorov
**Source:** "Springtime for Soviet Cinema"

## Definition
Rejection of Stalinist Grand Style. Smaller scale, individual subjectivity, everyday detail, broken narrative. Where Stalinism filmed "the people" as a geometric mass (Fall of Berlin's parade scenes), the New Wave films "the crowd as a union of distinct and diverse individuals" (Khutsiev's May Day Parade in *I Am Twenty*).

## Films using it
- [[09 The Cranes Are Flying]] — Veronika as flawed individual, not Stakhanovite Mother
- [[10 Ivan's Childhood]] — Ivan as broken child, not Pioneer hero
- [[11 Brief Encounters]] — Valentina as fragmented person, not unified Soviet citizen

## Example
Where Fall of Berlin (1949) ends with the geometric massed cheering of liberated Berlin — Stalinist monumentalism — Cranes (1957) ends with Veronika alone, holding flowers, the camera tracking her individual face as it registers Boris's death. Anti-monumentalism literally re-scales Soviet cinema from the parade to the face.
